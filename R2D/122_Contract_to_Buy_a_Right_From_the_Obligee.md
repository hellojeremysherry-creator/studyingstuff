§ 122 Contract to Buy a Right From the Obligee
A contract to purchase a right which the promisee has or may acquire against a third person is not 
within the Statute of Frauds as a contract to answer for the duty of another.
## Comments & Illustrations
### Comment
a.  Contract to buy.  Ordinarily a promise to buy a right and a promise to pay the debt of another are quite 
different transactions.  A promise to buy is not within the suretyship provision of the Statute of Frauds, but it 
may be within other provisions, particularly Uniform Commercial Code §§ 1-206, 8-319, 9-203.  See § 110; 
Statutory Note preceding § 110.
### Illustration
1.  D owes C $ 1,000 on open account.  S, who specializes in the purchase of slow accounts, orally promises to 
buy C's right against D for $ 800 if assignment is made within three months.  At the end of three months, C 
tenders S an assignment of the account.  S's promise is not within the suretyship provision of the Statute of 
Frauds.
b.  Suretyship in form of purchase.  Where a promise to buy a debt is conditional on the debtor's default and the 
amount to be paid is the same as if the debt had been guaranteed, the consequences of a contract to purchase 
and a contract of a surety are the same.  The distinction between a contract to buy and a contract of a surety 
does not lie in the formal difference in the words used but in the reality of the transaction.  For the purposes of 
the Statute of Frauds, the test is whether in all the circumstances the promisor is acquiring a right or protecting 
a creditor against a default.  Compare § 116.
### Illustration
2.  D corporation owes C $ 1,000 which is due.  S orally promises C that if C will grant D an extension of 60 
days, S will purchase the debt at that time if it is not then paid.  The circumstances indicate that S is really 
guaranteeing the account, and the promise is unenforceable.
## Reporter'S Notes
This Section is based on former § 190, but is not limited, as that Section was, to rights already possessed by 
the promisee.  See 3 Williston, Contracts § 480 (3d ed. 1960); 2 Corbin, Contracts § 392 (1950).
Comments a and b.  The Illustrations are based on the Illustrations to Restatement of Security § 99.  As to 
Illustration 2, see also Illustration 2 to former § 190.
