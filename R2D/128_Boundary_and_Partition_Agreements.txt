§ 128 Boundary and Partition Agreements
(1)  A contract between owners of adjoining tracts of land fixing a dividing boundary is within the 
Statute of Frauds but if the location of the boundary was honestly disputed the contract becomes 
enforceable notwithstanding the Statute when the agreed boundary has been marked or has been 
recognized in the subsequent use of the tracts.
(2)  A contract byjoint tenants or tenants in common to partition land into separate tracts for each 
tenant is within the Statute of Frauds but becomes enforceable notwithstanding the Statute as to each 
tract when possession of it is taken in severalty in accordance with the agreement.
COMMENTS & ILLUSTRATIONS 
Comment:
a.  Rationale.  Boundary and partition agreements have the effect of an agreement to convey land and are 
within the land contract provision of the Statute of Frauds.  The rules making them enforceable on the basis of 
action taken under the agreement are similar to the "part performance" doctrine stated in § 129.  In cases not 
within the rules of this Section, relief may be granted under the more general doctrine of § 129.
REPORTER'S NOTES
This Section is based on former § 196.  See 3 Williston, Contracts §§ 412-13 (3d ed. 1960); 2 Corbin, Contracts 
§ 490 (1950).
Comment a.  As to Subsection (1), see Smith v. Mefford, 243 Ark. 561, 420 S.W.2d 848 (1967); Johnston v. 
Monahan, 2 Wash. App. 452, 469 P.2d 930 (1970). As to Subsection (2), see Annot., 37 A.L.R.3d 962, 973-74 
(1971); see also Anderson v. Anderson, 521 P.2d 437 (Okl. Ct. App. 1974), holding that agreements to partition 
are not sales of an interest in real property, and thus are not within the Statute of Frauds, but then making an 
alternative holding that there had been sufficient part performance to make the agreement enforceable if the 
Statute were applicable.
