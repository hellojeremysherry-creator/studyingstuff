§ 142 Tort Liability for Acts Under Unenforceable Contract
Where because of the existence of a contract conduct would not be tortious, unenforceability of the 
contract under the Statute of Frauds does not make the conduct tortious if it occurs without notice of 
repudiation of the contract.
## Comments & Illustrations
### Comment
a.  Scope.  An unenforceable contract may include authority or consent to do acts which would otherwise 
constitute a tort.  The authority or consent is effective notwithstanding the Statute of Frauds to bar tort remedies 
for acts done pursuant to the contract, but the authority or consent may be revoked without liability.  Acts 
subsequent to revocation are not protected.
### Illustration
1.  A enters into an oral contract with B by which A promises to transfer Blackacre to B and B promises to pay $ 
5,000, B to have an immediate license to go upon land.  B does so.  A sues for trespass; B tenders $ 5,000 and 
demands a transfer.  A need not accept the money or make a transfer, but B has a good defense to A's action 
for trespass.
